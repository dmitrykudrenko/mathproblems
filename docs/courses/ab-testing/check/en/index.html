    What this check cannot see

    The page trusts your numbers and answers. Three things it cannot check, and each of them can flip the verdict:

    • Revenue per visitor. It is noisier than conversion, and one big order can decide the test (chapter 12). This check is for a "bought or not" rate only.
    • Sessions instead of people. If the tool splits people but counts sessions, noise looks like a signal (chapter 18). Enter people, not sessions.
    • The plan before the start. The size, the decision day and the segments are honest only when written down in advance (chapter 15). For the next test, start with a plan.
    How it is computed

    Traffic split: a chi-square test with one degree of freedom against the planned share; an alarm if the p-value is below 0.001, as in chapter 13.

    Result: a two-sided z-test for two proportions with a pooled rate; confidence = 1 − p. A 95% confidence interval for the difference in conversion, divided by A's conversion. With k slices and v new variants, each comparison's threshold is 5% divided by k·v, as in chapters 3 and 4.

    Chance to beat: a flat Beta(1, 1) starting assumption for both rates and numerical integration over a 4,000-point grid; the expected loss is the average of max(pA − pB, 0), in conversion points, as in chapter 11.

    Size: the sample formula of chapter 9 (two-sided 5%, 80% power, A's conversion as the base), with the planned share for an uneven split. Power is the share of tests of this size that would notice a lift of exactly the MDE.
